INTENSITY

OUTER IMAGE

Far from the mass the grid is untouched. Close in, every line is pushed outward and wraps around the ring. Deflection falls off as 1/θ, so the effect fades to nothing at the edges on its own — which is what makes it survivable behind body text.

INNER IMAGE

A point lens produces two images of everything. The second is inverted and compressed inside the Einstein radius — a mirrored copy of the entire plane, folded into the ring. This is the part almost nobody renders, and the reason the effect looks unfamiliar rather than like a warp filter.

SOURCE PROBE

The two accent dots are one source, imaged twice. Dot area tracks each image's magnification. As the source crosses behind the mass the pair stretches toward a full ring and magnification climbs — the geometry behind real microlensing surveys.

THE ENTIRE SIMULATION

θE  = √( 4GM/c² · DLS/(DL·DS) )        Einstein radius
β   = θ − θE²/θ                        lens equation
θ±  = ½( β ± √(β² + 4θE²) )            the two images
μ±  = ½[ (u²+2)/(u√(u²+4)) ± 1 ]     magnification,  u = β/θE

Four closed-form equations. No solver, no timestep, no ephemeris data — every frame is evaluated directly. Grid vertices are transformed as vectors rather than pixels, so lines stay a crisp 1 px and the whole thing runs in Canvas 2D with no WebGL and no dependencies.
